# ASD-STE100 Part 1 reference

Issue 9 (2025-01-15). Authority: https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf

Part 2 dictionary is **not** reproduced here. Use the PDF for approved words and meanings.

## Section 1 — Words

### [ste:1.1] Approved vocabulary

Use words that are: approved in the dictionary; technical nouns; or technical verbs.

*Example:* “use” (approved verb); “engine” (technical noun).

### [ste:1.2] Part of speech

Use approved dictionary words only as the specified part of speech.

*Example:* “test” is an approved noun, not an approved verb → “Do the leak test of the system,” not “Test the system for leaks.”

### [ste:1.3] Approved meaning

Use approved words only with their approved meanings.

### [ste:1.4] Verb and adjective forms

Use only the approved forms of verbs and adjectives (see dictionary tables).

### [ste:1.5] Technical noun categories

You may use words that fit a defined technical noun category (company/industry glossary).

### [ste:1.6] Non-dictionary words as technical nouns

Use a non-dictionary word only when it is (part of) a technical noun.

### [ste:1.7] Nouns as verbs

Do not use technical nouns as verbs.

### [ste:1.8] Approved technical nouns

Use technical nouns approved in your company, industry, or subject field.

### [ste:1.9] Short technical nouns

When choosing a technical noun, prefer short, easy-to-understand terms.

### [ste:1.10] No slang or jargon as technical nouns

Do not use regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns.

### [ste:1.11] One term per item

Do not use different technical nouns for the same item.

### [ste:1.12] Technical verb categories

You may use verbs that fit a defined technical verb category.

### [ste:1.13] Verbs as nouns

Do not use technical verbs as nouns.

### [ste:1.14] American English spelling

Use American English spelling unless another official directive applies.

## Section 2 — Multi-word nouns

### [ste:2.1] Length limit

Write multi-word nouns of no more than three words.

### [ste:2.2] Long technical nouns

When a technical noun has more than three words, write it in full (do not invent opaque abbreviations).

## Section 3 — Verbs

### [ste:3.1] Dictionary verb forms

Use only verb forms given in the dictionary.

### [ste:3.2] Allowed tenses

Use only the verb forms and tenses specified for STE (present, imperative, etc. per dictionary guidance).

### [ste:3.3] Past participle as adjective

You may use the past participle form as an adjective.

### [ste:3.4] No complex auxiliaries

Do not use auxiliary verbs to build complex verb constructions.

### [ste:3.5] -ing form

Use the “-ing” form only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.

### [ste:3.6] Active voice

Use the active voice. In descriptive writing, passive is allowed only when the agent is unknown.

### [ste:3.7] Verbs for actions

Use an approved verb to describe an action, not a noun or other part of speech.

## Section 4 — Sentences

### [ste:4.1] Short and clear

Write short and clear sentences.

### [ste:4.2] No omissions or contractions

Do not omit words or use contractions to shorten sentences.

### [ste:4.3] Vertical lists

Use a vertical list for complex text.

### [ste:4.4] Connecting words

Use connecting words and phrases to link related sentences.

### [ste:4.5] Articles and demonstratives

When applicable, use an article (`the`, `a`, `an`) or demonstrative (`this`, `these`) before a noun or multi-word noun.

## Section 5 — Procedural writing

### [ste:5.1] Sentence length (procedures)

Write short sentences. Maximum **20 words** per sentence in procedures.

### [ste:5.2] One instruction per sentence

Write only one instruction per sentence unless two or more actions occur at the same time.

### [ste:5.3] Imperative

Write instructions in the imperative (command) form.

### [ste:5.4] Condition before command

When the reader must know a condition first, start with a descriptive statement, then a comma, then the command.

### [ste:5.5] Notes vs instructions

Write notes to give information, not instructions.

## Section 6 — Descriptive writing

### [ste:6.1] Gradual information

Give information gradually.

### [ste:6.2] Key words and phrases

Use key words and phrases to show logical structure.

### [ste:6.3] Sentence length (descriptive)

Write short sentences. Maximum **25 words** per sentence in descriptive text.

### [ste:6.4] Paragraphs

Use paragraphs to group related information.

### [ste:6.5] One topic per paragraph

Each paragraph should have only one topic.

### [ste:6.6] Paragraph length

No paragraph should have more than six sentences.

## Section 7 — Safety instructions

### [ste:7.1] Risk level word

Use an applicable word (for example, “warning” or “caution”) to identify the level of risk.

### [ste:7.2] Command or condition first

Start a safety instruction with a clear, accurate command or condition.

### [ste:7.3] Explain risk

Give an explanation that shows the risk or possible result.

*Example pattern:* **WARNING:** DO NOT SWALLOW THE SOLVENT. … SOLVENTS ARE POISONOUS AND CAN CAUSE INJURY OR DEATH.

## Section 8 — Punctuation and word count

### [ste:8.1] Punctuation

You may use standard English punctuation except the **semicolon**.

### [ste:8.2] Hyphens

Use hyphens to connect directly related words.

### [ste:8.3] Parentheses

You may use parentheses for references, item IDs, abbreviations, alternatives, etc. (see PDF for full list).

### [ste:8.4] Colon in lists

In a vertical list, a colon counts like a period for word-count purposes and ends the introductory sentence.

### [ste:8.5] Parentheses word count

Text in parentheses counts as **one word** in that sentence.

### [ste:8.6] Single-word elements

Count each number, unit, abbreviation, alphanumeric ID, quoted text, title/heading/placard, and proper noun as one word.

### [ste:8.7] Hyphenated words

Hyphenated words count as one word.

## Section 9 — Writing practices

### [ste:9.1] Sentence reconstruction

When word-for-word dictionary replacement is insufficient, rewrite the sentence (change structure).

### [ste:9.2] Correct approved words

Use each approved word correctly.

### [ste:9.3] No phrasal verbs

When two words go together, do not form phrasal verbs.

### [ste:9.4] Consistent style

Use consistent terminology and wording throughout a document.

### General recommendations (GR)

Part 1 also lists GR-1 through GR-8 (conjunction “that”, preposition “with”, pronouns, “this”, false friends, Latin abbreviations, inclusive language, possessives). Consult the PDF when those constructions appear.
